          ATLAS

          Security

          Nothing can be stolen because nothing exists to steal.

          When you connect a bank, wallet, or card, Atlas never stores the account number, the sort code, or a copy of your credentials. The connection is compressed into a single cryptographic attestation, bound permanently to your identity's keypair.

          • 01Your identity is a keypair you generate and hold. Atlas never sees the private key.
          • 02Each source you connect becomes a signed attestation, not a stored copy of your credentials.
          • 03Every attestation can be revoked instantly and independently, without touching the others.
          • 04Everything stored on this device, identity, sources, activity, is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key generated and held on this device. No password to create or remember, and nothing sits on disk as plain, readable text.

          There is no central account database to breach and no password to phish, because neither exists inside the protocol. There is only a signed proof: verifiable by anyone, forgeable by no one.

          The last 1%

          A few things currently keep this under 100%.

          Optional recovery. Off by default. If you enable it, 5 recovery keys are generated on your device, once, and they become the only way back into that identity. Choosing this trades a sliver of the purest anonymous state, one with no recovery path at all, for the ability to get back in if you lose access. It can be turned on. It can never be turned off.

          Hosting infrastructure. Running a service on the internet means a request still has to reach a server somewhere, and that layer sees the same ordinary connection data any web host sees. It never sees what happens after: every identity, source, and activity record is AES-256-GCM encrypted before it ever touches storage, under a key generated and held on your device alone. It's never transmitted anywhere, so nothing outside this device, including us, can decrypt what's stored here.

          Local device access. The encryption key lives on your device so there's no password to create or remember. That protects your data if it's ever copied or intercepted somewhere else, but not from someone holding this device itself, open and unlocked. No app can fix that. Securing the device is on you.

          We are working toward being the first 100% anonymous financial system: no optional trade-off left to make, and nothing seen at the infrastructure layer beyond the bare minimum a connection requires.
